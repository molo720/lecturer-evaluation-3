# RQ4 Formative Pilot: Lecturer and Administrator Report Usefulness

**Status: Protocol prepared; no participant session or feedback has been recorded in this repository. Do not write anticipated feedback as study findings.**

## Purpose

RQ4 asks how intended users find the reports useful for interpretation and potential teaching enhancement. A small informal session can identify confusing labels and presentation issues. With only two or three participants, it is formative feedback, not a statistically valid usability study and not validation of teaching effectiveness.

## Preparation checklist

- [ ] Invite 2-3 departmental colleagues or supervisors who are willing to provide feedback.
- [ ] Explain the prototype, that displayed comments and counts are synthetic/engineered, and that the system is not for high-stakes staff decisions.
- [ ] Ask for consent before taking notes or recording any comments. Do not collect names, matriculation numbers, personal staff information, or real student data.
- [ ] Use one fixed version of the dashboard and record the deployment URL, date, and commit/release identifier if known.
- [ ] Give each participant the same tasks and the same synthetic example report.

## Standard tasks

1. Open the dashboard and identify the total evaluation count and mean SET rating.
2. Open a lecturer report and explain in their own words what one aspect row's positive, neutral, and negative counts represent.
3. Find an aspect marked “Insufficient evidence” and explain why that is different from “Balanced / Neutral”.
4. Open Model Evaluation and describe what accuracy and Macro-F1 say, including why those metrics do not establish accuracy on authentic student feedback.
5. Identify one report element that could be misunderstood or lead to an unfair conclusion.

Do not ask participants to judge or rate a real lecturer. The example interface must be clearly labelled as synthetic demonstration data.

## Interview questions

Ask each participant the same open questions:

1. Which page or chart was easiest to understand, and why?
2. Which label, number, chart, or summary was confusing?
3. Does the report make the difference between an overall comment sentiment and an aspect-level sentiment clear?
4. What does “Insufficient evidence” mean to you? What would you expect the report to do when no aspect is detected?
5. Could any displayed summary be unfairly over-interpreted? What warning would help?
6. Would this kind of report be useful as a starting point for a teaching-development discussion? Why or why not?
7. What single change would you prioritise before a wider pilot?

## Coding the feedback

After the session, group notes into a small number of themes such as interpretation clarity, trust in sentiment labels, usefulness of summaries, concern about false positives/negation, and desired features. Support each theme with anonymised notes from the actual session. Do not include a theme unless at least one participant expressed evidence for it. Report disagreements rather than forcing a consensus.

## Suggested write-up after data collection

“An informal formative session was conducted with [N] participants ([roles]) on [date] using the synthetic demonstration dashboard. Participants completed [tasks actually completed]. The main observations were [themes supported by notes]. These comments were used to prioritise [specific changes]. Because the session was small, purposive, and based on synthetic data, it provides preliminary qualitative feedback only; it does not establish general user acceptance or institutional readiness.”

If no session occurs before submission, say explicitly that RQ4 remains unanswered and that this protocol is a planned next step. Do not fill the table or paragraph with invented names, dates, quotations, task outcomes, or themes.
