# Small Formative User-Feedback Pilot for RQ4

**Status: protocol only. No pilot participants or responses are recorded in this file.** Complete this record only after the sessions have actually taken place.

## Purpose and boundary

RQ4 asks how useful lecturers and academic administrators find the reports for understanding student feedback. This optional formative pilot is intended to uncover basic interpretation and usability issues, not to establish representative user acceptance or teaching effectiveness.

Invite two or three departmental colleagues/supervisors who voluntarily agree to participate. Do not imply that the assistant conducted these sessions. Use a demo account and synthetic/demo data only. Do not import real student records for the pilot. Do not use the output for employment, promotion, disciplinary or other high-stakes decisions.

## Participant information and consent

Read this before the task:

> You are invited to try a prototype that combines numerical rating summaries with text-sentiment indicators. It has been evaluated primarily with controlled computer-generated/template data, and it has not been validated for high-stakes institutional decisions. Taking part is voluntary. You may stop at any time. Please do not enter real student names, matriculation numbers, contact details or other identifying information. I will record only a participant code, role category, brief task observations and your feedback. Your comments will be summarised without your name.

Record verbal/written consent only according to your department's requirements. If institutional or supervisor approval is required for user feedback involving research participants, obtain it before running the session.

## Tasks

Ask each participant to think aloud while completing these tasks, without coaching them through the answers.

1. Open a lecturer report and identify the mean SET rating and number of comments.
2. Find one aspect labelled as a strength or improvement area and explain which counts support that label.
3. Find an aspect with limited or insufficient evidence. Explain what the label means and whether the report gives enough information to reach a conclusion.
4. Interpret a mixed comment that is positive about Teaching Clarity but negative about Assessment and Grading.
5. Explain one action they would take after seeing the report, and what extra evidence they would need before acting.

For each task, record completion (Completed / Partially completed / Not completed), approximate time if measured, observed confusion, and any incorrect interpretation. Do not infer the participant's opinion from facial expression or silence.

## Reporting after the sessions

After completing all sessions, summarise:

- Number invited, number participated, role categories and session dates (do not list names).
- Which tasks participants completed and recurring points of confusion.
- Rating distributions per item. With only two or three participants, do not present percentages as stable population estimates.
- Qualitative themes, how many participants raised each theme, and a brief anonymised paraphrase for each theme. Do not invent quotes; use quotation marks only for actual notes recorded with consent.
- Changes made in response to feedback, with evidence and a retest record.
- Limitations: convenience sample, small number, informal tasks, potential facilitator bias, synthetic demo data, and no claim of generalisability.

Only after this work is conducted may RQ4 be described as partially explored by a small formative pilot. The pilot still would not establish broad institutional acceptance or validate model accuracy.
